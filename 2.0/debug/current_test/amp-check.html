Amp Check

Two measurements with the multimeter: how much current the motor eats, vs how much the Arduino itself sips. The trick both times: current is measured in series — the meter becomes part of the wire, so electricity flows through it.

⚠ Before touching the dial

  • Move the red probe to the 10A socket on the meter (not the mA one — the motor's current can blow the mA fuse). Dial to DC amps (A⎓).
  • The meter goes in the middle of a broken wire — never across two power pins. Across = short circuit = blown fuse (or worse).
  • When you're done, move the red probe back to the V socket. Leaving it on 10A and later measuring a battery's voltage is the classic way to kill a meter.

The takeaway: the motor wants ~0.24 A all the time — even holding still, because the driver keeps the coils energized. A supply that can't sustain that sags, and you saw exactly what that looks like: dim light, buzz, no motion. Same wiring, same motor, same code — the only variable was the supply. That's real electrical debugging.
