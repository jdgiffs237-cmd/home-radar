Stepper Upgrade Wiring

The 28BYJ-48 stepper motor + its green ULN2003 driver board, steered by the joystick — everything powered from the Arduino's 5V (USB). Six jumper wires plus one capacitor, and the motor's own cable just clicks into the driver's white socket.

Why D2–D5?

Deliberately avoids D9/D10/D11, which the radar uses for the servo and the ultrasonic sensor — so later the stepper and the sensor can be wired at the same time.

Servo vs stepper

A servo is told an angle; a stepper is told "take N steps" and doesn't know where it is. Precise and repeatable (2048 steps per turn ≈ 0.18° each!), but it needs a known starting position — point it straight ahead by hand before powering up.

The USB power budget

A USB port gives ~500 mA. Motor 240 + Arduino 50 + sensors ~20 ≈ 310 mA — it fits, with modest headroom. Two rules keep it happy: the capacitor stays in (it absorbs the motor's split-second spikes), and the sketch releases the coils when idle so the motor only drinks while actually moving. If things ever get flaky, plug into a rear PC port or a powered hub rather than a laptop's side port.
