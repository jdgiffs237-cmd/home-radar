Five Equations You Watched Happen

You built the radar first, on purpose. So none of this is abstract — each equation is the math behind something your own sensor already did on the desk.

1

Range is time

R = c · t / 2   c = 343 m/s (sound)

Send a chirp, wait for the echo, measure how long the round trip took. Distance is just that time times the speed of sound, halved (the sound went there and back). That single line is the entire sensor.

You saw it: the debug line R 18 90 0.091 — your hand threw an echo back in about 0.5 ms, and the Arduino turned that time into 9 cm. The 2.7 m wall readings were the same math on a longer round trip.
2

Range resolution is pulse width

ΔR = c · τ / 2   ≈ 3.4 cm for HC-SR04

Two objects closer together than half the pulse length blur into one blob. The HC-SR04's chirp is 200 microseconds long, which sets a hard floor of about 3.4 cm. No software trick beats it — two things 2 cm apart in depth are one dot, forever.

You saw it: the median-of-3 pings never resolved fine detail on a surface — it can't. This is the sensor's grain, baked into physics, not a tuning problem.
3

Angular resolution is beamwidth

ΔX ≈ R · θ   θ ≈ 30° for HC-SR04

Your beam isn't a laser line — it's a wide ~30° cone. Two targets at the same distance but only a few degrees apart both sit inside the cone at once, so they return as one smeared arc. The farther out you look, the wider that blur gets.

You saw it twice. Objects painted as arcs, never points — that's the cone. And when your hand got close, it lit up every bearing at once: up close the cone swallows the whole field, so "the target is where I'm pointing" stopped being true. That's why the $7 VL53L0X laser (2° beam) is such an upgrade.
4

Scan pattern & revisit time

revisit = steps × time-per-step

To see everywhere you must sweep, and sweeping takes time. However long between two looks at the same bearing is the revisit time — and anything can move, unseen, in that gap. Sweep wide and each spot updates slowly; park narrow and one spot updates fast. You can't have both.

You felt it in your thumb: the ghost dots that lingered where your hand used to be — stale until you swept back. And the choice between panning the whole room vs. parking on the doorway is radar's search-vs-track tradeoff. Billion-dollar systems fight this exact fight; you fight it with a joystick.
5

Echo strength falls off a cliff

Preceived ∝ σ / R⁴

The term that matters: R⁴. Double the distance and the echo you get back drops by 16×. That's why targets don't fade politely with range — they're fine, then suddenly gone. And σ (how well a shape reflects, not how big it is) is why a wall facing you square is bright but the same wall at an angle vanishes.

You saw it: your wall only appeared at the one or two bearings facing it dead-on — steer a few degrees off and it disappeared, because the echo bounced sideways into the room instead of back to you. Soft things (clothes, the carpet) barely showed at all. That's σ and R⁴ — the same physics that shapes stealth aircraft.

Where this goes next
