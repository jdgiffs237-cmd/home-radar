Reading the Scope

The green half-circle is a map of your room, drawn from above, with you at the bottom. Here's every mark on it, decoded.

The display, annotated

echo (something there) strong / near return empty or unexplored

The five rules

  1. You are the bottom-center dot. The scope is your room from above; straight up on screen is straight ahead of the sensor (90°).
  2. Out = away. Each ring is one metre. A dot on the third ring is something 3 m from the sensor at that bearing.
  3. The long arc is your wall. A wall returns at every bearing, so it paints as a broad arc. It's the permanent scenery — radar people call it clutter.
  4. A target is a dot inside the wall's arc. Something closer than the scenery at that bearing. Hold your hand in front of the sensor and a fresh dot appears well inside the arc.
  5. Dark wedges are the unknown, not the empty. With the joystick firmware, a bearing stays dark until you steer the beam across it — and it shows the last echo until you revisit. If something moved since you looked, the scope is showing a memory. Steering back refreshes it.
The beam is wide. The HC-SR04 hears a ~30° cone, so one object smears across several neighbouring bearings — your hand paints a short arc, not a single dot. That's physics, not a bug (and it's why the VL53L0X laser upgrade exists).

Three experiments to make it click

  1. Paint the room. Hold the stick gently to one side and let the beam crawl the full 0°–180°. Watch the wall's arc assemble dot by dot. That arc is a floor plan you just drew with sound.
  2. Be the target. Steer to center, hold your palm ~40 cm in front, and watch a bright dot appear far inside the wall. Move your hand out slowly — the dot slides outward ring by ring.
  3. Catch the scope lying. Paint your hand at one bearing, then pull your hand away without moving the stick off to that side again. The dot stays — a ghost of where your hand was. Steer across it and it clears. You've just experienced revisit time, a real problem real radar operators manage.

When the auto-sweep firmware (radar_sweep.ino) is loaded instead, the servo does the painting for you, the whole picture refreshes every couple of seconds, and no wedge stays stale for long — that's the trade: the machine sweeps everywhere slowly, your thumb looks anywhere instantly.
