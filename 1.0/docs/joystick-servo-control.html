Joystick Servo Control

Two levels, both on battery power. Level 1: the stick drives the servo, nothing else — a playground for the steering code. Level 2: add the sensor and hand-steer a live radar beam (radar_joystick.ino v3: paints while you pan, filters while you park).

Level 1 · stick + servo, battery powered

Firmware: firmware/joystick_servo/joystick_servo.ino. The power module feeds the breadboard rails from the 9V battery; the servo and joystick drink from the rails; the Uno stays on USB and shares only its ground. (If the HC-SR04 is already wired in from Level 2, leave it — this sketch simply ignores it.)

Run it: upload joystick_servo.ino (hands off the stick for the first second — it measures the stick's center then). The servo snaps to 90° and obeys the stick. Knobs at the top of the sketch: MAX_SPEED, DEADZONE, JOY_DIRECTION.

Level 2 · all three modules + battery power

Three modules share power — this is where the kit's breadboard and Power Supply Module earn their place. The breadboard's long red and blue edge rows ("rails") are connected all the way along; the power module snaps onto the end of them and feeds them from the 9V battery. The servo's current spikes now happen on the battery's rail and never touch the Uno's USB power — which is what was faking those super-close targets.

Rewiring from the old setup? One wire must be removed: the Uno 5V → red rail jumper. The power module owns the red rail now, and two 5V sources fighting each other can damage both. Ground stays shared. (A 100–470 µF capacitor across the rails is still a fine extra if you ever buy some — but with battery power it's optional, not needed.)

Run it

  1. Open firmware/radar_joystick/radar_joystick.ino in Arduino IDE and Upload (Board: Arduino Uno). Close the Serial Monitor afterwards.
  2. python run_live.py — it finds the Arduino by itself.
  3. Steer with the stick; the scope paints whatever bearings you cross. Sweep the whole room once, then park the beam on a doorway and wait for someone to walk through it.
How v3 pings — two modes, like a real radar: slow-to-medium pan (up to about ¾ stick) fires one quick ping per step, so the picture paints live as you sweep — that's search mode. Parked (stick released) switches to median-of-3 pings for a clean, filtered reading — track mode. Full-stick slewing doesn't ping at all: a hard-driven head vibrates and hears its own ringing as phantom close targets. So: sweep at a relaxed pace to paint, park to sharpen, slam the stick only to reposition.
Moved the mount? Two rules. (1) The scope is a memory — re-sweep after any move or you're looking at the old room. (2) If you turned the rig around so the servo's 0° end points to your left, everything reads mirrored; set MOUNT_FLIPPED = true at the top of the sketch and both the scope and the stick flip back to matching the room.

Why v2 doesn't lie anymore

v1 sometimes showed everything jumping unrealistically close. Two culprits, two fixes:

  • Vibration ghosts: a moving servo shakes the sensor, and the transducer hears its own ringing as an echo centimetres away. Fix: the settle gate — no pings until the head has been still for SETTLE_MS (60 ms).
  • Power-sag glitches: the servo's current spikes dip the 5V rail and corrupt single readings. Fixes: the power module moves the servo onto the 9V battery so spikes never touch the Uno, and each stored range is now the median of 3 pings, so one surviving bad reading gets outvoted by the two honest ones.

The stick itself is unchanged: each axis is a potentiometer read as 0–1023 (~512 at rest), and the sketch nudges the servo angle proportionally 50 times a second — rate control, same scheme as a drone transmitter.

Tuning knobs

  • JOY_DIRECTION — flip to -1 if push-right steers left (the module is just rotated in your hand).
  • MAX_SPEED — degrees per step at full push; raise for twitchier steering.
  • DEADZONE — how far off-center counts as "pushed"; raise it if the servo creeps on its own.
  • MOUNT_FLIPPED — true if the servo's 0° end points to your left (rig turned around). Mirrors the servo and the stick together.
  • PAN_PING_MAX_STEP — how fast you can pan and still get pings (degrees per loop). Raise it to paint at higher speeds; lower it if ghosts creep in while sweeping.
  • SETTLE_MS — how long the head must be still to count as parked (switches to the filtered median-of-3 mode).
