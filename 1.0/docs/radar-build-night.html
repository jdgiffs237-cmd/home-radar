Radar Build Night

Five steps from a box of parts to your room on a radar scope. Tick each step as you land it — this page remembers where you are.

Set the VL53L0X aside tonight. The wiring and firmware below are for the ultrasonic sensor. The laser is the upgrade we do after the basic radar works — it gets its own evening.

Use the male-to-female jumper wires from the kit (pin on one end, socket on the other). Every wire below plugs a module pin straight to an Arduino header pin — no breadboard needed for v1.

The classic mistake: TRIG and ECHO swapped. Everything powers up, the servo sweeps happily, and every range reads 0 or max. If that's your symptom later, swap these two wires first.

  • Tape the sensor to the servo's plastic arm (the "horn"), eyes facing outward.
  • Tape the servo body to something heavy — a book, a mug of coins. Tape is genuinely the right tool for v1.
  • Keep the sensor centered over the servo's spin axis, not hanging out in front — an offset bends every range reading.
  • Clear 20 cm in front of the sensor: no cables, no desk edge, no servo wire drooping into view.

  • Install Arduino IDE 2 (free).
  • Plug the Uno into USB, open firmware/radar_sweep/radar_sweep.ino from the project folder.
  • Tools → Board → Arduino Uno. Tools → Port → the COM port that appeared.
  • Hit the → Upload button. The board's lights flicker while it flashes.
No COM port listed? Your board needs the CH340 USB driver — a two-minute fix. Ask Claude and we'll sort it.

Tools → Serial Monitor, set the speed to 115200 baud. The servo starts sweeping and lines scroll past:

# radar_sweep v1 fov=180 step=5 c=343.0
S 0
R 0 5 1.834      ← cell 0, angle 5°, echo at 1.83 m
R 1 10 1.821
R 2 15 -1.000    ← -1 means no echo came back
...
E 0 37

Now wave your hand in front of it and watch the numbers drop to your hand's distance. That's the whole project in miniature: pulse out, echo back, time-of-flight to range.

Close the Serial Monitor first — Windows gives one program the port at a time, and Python can't open it while the IDE holds it. This catches everyone once.

python -m serial.tools.list_ports    ← find your COM number
python run_live.py --port COM3       ← swap in yours

Green sweeping scope, your actual desk and walls painted on it. Stand up, move around, watch yourself appear.

When it misbehaves

SymptomCause
All ranges read 0 or maxTRIG and ECHO wires swapped.
Board resets mid-sweepServo current spikes browning out USB power. Put one of the kit's electrolytic capacitors (100–470 µF) across the servo's red and brown wires — stripe side (−) to brown.
Every range 3–5% offSpeed of sound vs room temperature. Tune SPEED_OF_SOUND in radar/config.py.
Targets smeared across 20°Partly the 30° beam (real life), partly pinging while the servo's still moving — raise SERVO_SETTLE_MS.
Ghost targets at fixed rangesLast ping's echo off a far wall arriving during this ping. Ping interval too short.
Soft things invisibleClothing eats ultrasound. Not a bug — hold up a book instead.
Nothing beyond ~2 mNormal. The 4 m spec assumes a big flat wall dead ahead.
