The Range Flip

What range_to_intensity in detect.py is actually doing, and why the two special cases exist.

01The problem: your sensor speaks upside-down

CFAR hunts for peaks — cells whose value sticks up above their neighbours. But a rangefinder reports distance, so the most interesting thing in the room (a person standing close) produces the smallest number. Targets are valleys, not peaks.

02The flip: range and intensity share one ruler

Fix the sensor ceiling at max_range = 4.0 m. Any reading splits that ruler in two: the part the echo travelled (range) and the part left over (intensity). They always sum to 4.0 — intensity is just "how far inside the ceiling this return landed."

intensity = max_range − range

03The three cases

The formula only applies in the middle of the ruler. Off either end, the answer is forced to 0.0 — for two different reasons.

range < 0 · miss −1.0 → 0.0

The sensor saw nothing. Naive flip gives 5.0, a monster false target. Absence of evidence maps to zero intensity.

0 ≤ range ≤ max 0.5 → 3.5

The real formula: max_range − range. Close return, big intensity.

range > max · clamp 4.7 → 0.0

Naive flip goes negative (−0.7). Beyond the ceiling there's nothing to detect, so clamp to zero.

05So the three lines are…

Conceptually: flip everything, then zero out both illegal ends. In numpy that's the subtraction, plus one masking step that sends misses and beyond-ceiling readings to 0.0 (have a look at np.where or np.clip — mind that a miss must not go through the subtraction first and survive). The doctest in the docstring is your check: [−1.0, 0.5, 3.5] → [0.0, 3.5, 0.5].
